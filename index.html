About

London-based type designer Mor Solymosi develops original typefaces and typographic systems for public information, transport, editorial and digital environments. His practice brings together letterform design, spacing, language support, symbols and pictograms as connected parts of a coherent visual language.

Mor approaches typeface development as both a formal and functional discipline. Each family is shaped through careful drawing, optical adjustment and extensive testing across weights, sizes and applications. Particular attention is given to rhythm, character differentiation, open counters and consistent proportions, helping the type remain distinctive while supporting clarity and legibility.

His work spans custom typeface families, variable fonts, display and text styles, character-set development, diacritics, kerning, OpenType features and integrated pictogram systems. By considering typography from individual glyphs through to complete information systems, Mor creates typefaces that retain a clear identity across print, screens, signage and spatial applications.

MS Relay Sans system alignment

This page shows how the main components of MS Relay Sans operate together as a unified information system. Letterforms, numerals, pictograms and directional symbols are shown at the same scale, allowing their proportions, visual weight and overall character to be compared directly.

Typography and symbols are designed to share a consistent optical language. Stroke weight, spacing, scale and internal proportions are balanced so that pictograms and symbols sit naturally alongside text without becoming visually dominant or disconnected from the typographic system.

The composition also demonstrates how these relationships remain consistent across different forms of information, including destinations, directions, numbering and identification. Each element retains its individual function while contributing to a common visual hierarchy.

Pictograms are adjusted optically according to their form and complexity rather than being forced into a rigid construction grid. Their proportions and visual weight are developed in direct relation to the typeface, allowing text and symbols to operate together across signage, maps, printed information and digital interfaces.

MS Relay Sans numeral study

Numerals form an essential part of a wayfinding typeface, often carrying information as important as the words around them. Platform numbers, exits, routes, gates, floors and distances all depend on fast and unambiguous numerical recognition.

Shown here in the Thin weight, the numerals demonstrate the underlying construction of MS Relay Sans with minimal visual weight. Their proportions and individual forms are designed for clear differentiation while maintaining a consistent relationship with the alphabet.

At this scale, the structure, spacing and rhythm of the numerals can be examined individually and in sequence, showing how numerical information operates as an integral part of the wider system.

MS Relay Sans is structured across ten weights, ranging from Thin to Black. The range allows information hierarchy to be established through weight while maintaining consistent proportions and character across the family.

Lighter weights support editorial and larger-scale applications, while the heavier weights provide the visual presence required for directional information, identification and signage. Each weight remains structurally consistent, allowing different levels of information to operate within the same typographic system.

This page shows MS Relay Sans in a selection of words drawn from transport and wayfinding environments. At this scale, the individual character of the letterforms and their relationship to one another can be observed while legibility remains the primary consideration.

Distinctive details help prevent vertical forms from becoming visually repetitive or merging into continuous strokes. Characters such as the l and t therefore use more identifiable forms, supporting faster recognition within directional information.

Wayfinding elements

This grotesk typeface has been developed as a functional and adaptable visual system for wayfinding, transport and public-information environments. Its construction is based on clear proportions, open counters and carefully controlled stroke relationships, allowing the letterforms to remain legible across a wide range of scales, viewing distances and applications.

While the overall character is neutral and systematic, subtle details prevent the design from appearing mechanical or anonymous. The terminals, joins and curved forms have been optically adjusted to create a consistent rhythm, while the spacing supports both short directional messages and longer passages of information.

The accompanying pictogram family extends the same visual language through shared proportions, stroke weight, geometry and formal details. Across the following pages, the typeface and symbols are presented through selected characters, words, directional messages, pictograms, spacing studies and practical wayfinding applications.

This chapter of the study focuses on the coherence between the typography and the pictogram system, examining how both elements work together as a unified visual language.

These examples test MS Relay Sans across a range of wayfinding layouts, from simple destination signs to more complex combinations of directions, exits, transport information and landmarks.

The information structure and basic signage logic are based on established attractions-signage standards, while the visual treatment has been updated through the MS Relay Sans system. This reflects one of the typeface’s intended roles: to retain familiar wayfinding conventions while making the overall appearance feel more current, consistent and easier to manage.

As information density increases, typography, arrows and transport symbols are organised through scale, spacing and alignment to establish a clearer hierarchy. The updated system is designed to remain legible and structured even when a sign contains a large amount of information, helping users scan, understand and navigate the content more efficiently.
